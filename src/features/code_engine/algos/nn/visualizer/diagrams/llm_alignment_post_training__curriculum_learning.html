Curriculum Learning

In classical empirical risk minimization, training batches are sampled uniformly and independently from an invariant data distribution $\mathcal{D}$. However, in complex high-dimensional sequence modeling, exposing randomly initialized models to extremely long, noisy, or linguistically intricate examples induces unstable gradient variance and slow optimization dynamics. <strong>Curriculum Learning</strong> imposes an explicit non-stationary data schedule ordered by difficulty, presenting simple, clean concepts early before progressively introducing harder, more nuanced distributions. Let each training example $x \in \mathcal{D}$ possess a difficulty metric $d(x) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ (e.g., sequence length $|x|$, loss under a small reference model $\mathcal{L}_{\text{ref}}(x)$, or parse-tree depth). A <strong>Pacing Function</strong> $g: [0, T_{\text{pace}}] \to [\gamma_0, 1.0]$ governs the fraction of easiest training examples available to the optimizer at training step $t$: {\boldmath
◈ PRIMARY MATHEMATICAL FORMULATION & DYNAMIC FLOW KaTeX Verified · Closed-Form
$$\mathcal{D}_t = \left\{ x \in \mathcal{D} \;\middle|\; d(x) \le F_d^{-1}(g(t)) \right\}$$

◈ Algorithmic Execution Pipeline
ALGORITHM Curriculum Pacing and Batch Eligibility Filter
Require: Sample difficulties $\mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{R}^N$, step $t$, pacing duration $T_{\text{pace}}$, mode $pacing\_type$, initial fraction $\gamma_0$
Ensure: Eligible indices $\mathcal{I}_{\text{elig}}$, threshold $d_{\text{thresh}}$, active fraction $f$, sorted indices $\mathcal{I}_{\text{sort}}$
1 $progress \gets \min(1.0, t / T_{\text{pace}})$
2 if $progress \ge 1.0$ then
3 $f \gets 1.0$
4 else if $pacing\_type == \text{"linear"}$ then
5 $f \gets \gamma_0 + (1.0 - \gamma_0) \cdot progress$
6 else if $pacing\_type == \text{"root"}$ then
7 $f \gets \gamma_0 + (1.0 - \gamma_0) \cdot \sqrt{progress}$
8 else if $pacing\_type == \text{"exponential"}$ then
9 $f \gets \gamma_0 \cdot (1.0 / \gamma_0)^{progress}$
10 else
11 $f \gets \gamma_0 + (1.0 - \gamma_0) \cdot progress$
12 $f \gets \max(\gamma_0, \min(1.0, f))$
13 Sort pairs $(i, \mathbf{d}[i])$ ascending by difficulty to produce sorted indices $\mathcal{I}_{\text{sort}}$
14 $count \gets \max(1, \min(N, \lceil f \cdot N \rceil))$
15 $\mathcal{I}_{\text{elig}} \gets \mathcal{I}_{\text{sort}}[0 : count]$
16 $d_{\text{thresh}} \gets \mathbf{d}[\mathcal{I}_{\text{elig}}[count - 1]]$
17 return $(\mathcal{I}_{\text{elig}}, d_{\text{thresh}}, count / N, \mathcal{I}_{\text{sort}})$

★ Theoretical Guarantees & Invariant Bounds
Monotonic Convex Hull Expansion of Curriculum Datasets
Let $g(t)$ be a monotonically non-decreasing pacing function: $g(t_1) \le g(t_2)$ for all $t_1 \le t_2$. Then the sequence of active datasets forms a monotonic filtration: $$ \mathcal{D}_{t_1} \subseteq \mathcal{D}_{t_2} \subseteq \mathcal{D} $$ and the convex hull of active data distributions expands monotonically: $\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal{D}_{t_1}) \subseteq \operatorname{conv}(\mathcal{D}_{t_2})$.
Variance Reduction Bound Under Initial Low-Difficulty Warmup
Let gradient noise covariance be proportional to sample loss: $\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla \ell(x) - \nabla L\|^2] \le C \cdot d(x)$. Then the initial gradient variance under curriculum learning is bounded by $\gamma_0 \cdot \operatorname{Var}_{\text{uniform}}$, strictly reducing initial optimization noise.
