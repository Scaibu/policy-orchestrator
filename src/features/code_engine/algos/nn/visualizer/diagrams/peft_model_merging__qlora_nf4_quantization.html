Qlora Nf4 Quantization

Fine-tuning 65B+ parameter language models in 16-bit precision requires over 780 GB of GPU memory across multiple multi-GPU nodes. <strong>QLoRA (Quantized Low-Rank Adaptation)</strong> compresses frozen base models to a 4-bit representation without quality degradation, enabling the fine-tuning of a 65B parameter model on a single 48 GB GPU. QLoRA introduces three coordinated algorithmic innovations: \begin{enumerate} \item <strong>NormalFloat4 (NF4) Quantization</strong>: A theoretically optimal 4-bit data type for normally distributed neural network weights $W \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$. The 16 discrete NF4 grid points $\{q_i\}_{i=0}^{15} \subset [-1, 1]$ correspond to exact quantiles of the standard normal distribution $\mathcal{N}(0, 1)$: {\boldmath

◈ Algorithmic Execution Pipeline
ALGORITHM QLoRA NF4 Block Quantization, Dequantization, and Forward Map
Require: Raw weights $\mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times k}$, input $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^k$, adapter matrices $\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B}$, block size $B_{\text{size}}$, scale $\alpha$, rank $r$
Ensure: Indices $\mathbf{I} \in \{0, \dots, 15\}^{d \times k}$, scales $\mathbf{s}$, dequantized $\tilde{\mathbf{W}}$, output $\mathbf{h}$, error $\mathcal{E}$
1 $N \gets d \cdot k, \quad num\_blocks \gets \lceil N / B_{\text{size}} \rceil, \quad s_{\text{lora}} \gets \alpha / r$
2 Allocate $\mathbf{I} \in \mathbb{Z}^N, \quad \tilde{\mathbf{W}} \in \mathbb{R}^N, \quad \mathbf{s} \in \mathbb{R}^{num\_blocks}$
3 for $b = 0$ \textbf{to} $num\_blocks - 1$ do
4 $start \gets b \cdot B_{\text{size}}, \quad end \gets \min(N, (b + 1) \cdot B_{\text{size}})$
5 $scale \gets \max_{j \in [start, end)} |\mathbf{W}[j]|$
6 $\mathbf{s}[b] \gets scale$
7 for $j = start$ \textbf{to} $end - 1$ do
8 $norm\_w \gets \mathbf{W}[j] / (scale + 10^{-12})$
9 $idx^* \gets \arg\min_{i \in [0, 15]} |norm\_w - \operatorname{NF4\_Table}[i]|$
10 $\mathbf{I}[j] \gets idx^*, \quad \tilde{\mathbf{W}}[j] \gets scale \cdot \operatorname{NF4\_Table}[idx^*]$
11 $\mathcal{E} \gets \frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1} (\mathbf{W}[j] - \tilde{\mathbf{W}}[j])^2$
12 Allocate $\mathbf{h} \in \mathbb{R}^d$
13 for $i = 0$ \textbf{to} $d-1$ do
14 $base\_dot \gets \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \tilde{\mathbf{W}}[i \cdot k + j] \cdot \mathbf{x}[j]$
15 $ax \gets \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \mathbf{A}[\cdot, j] \cdot \mathbf{x}[j]$
16 $\mathbf{h}[i] \gets base\_dot + s_{\text{lora}} \cdot \sum_{r'} \mathbf{B}[i, r'] \cdot ax[r']$
17 return $(\mathbf{I}, \mathbf{s}, \tilde{\mathbf{W}}, \mathbf{h}, \mathcal{E})$

★ Theoretical Guarantees & Invariant Bounds
Information-Theoretic Optimality of NormalFloat4 Under Gaussian Priors
Let $X \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ be a continuous standard normal random variable. The 16-level quantile partition $q_i = Q_X(p_i)$ achieves equal information entropy: $$ H(q) = -\sum_{i=0}^{15} P(X \in \operatorname{bin}_i) \log_2 P(X \in \operatorname{bin}_i) = 4.0 \text{ bits} $$ maximizing the Shannon entropy of the 4-bit representation.
Zero-Mean Preservation Property of Asymmetric NF4 Grid
Because $q_7 = 0.0$ is an exact grid point in the NF4 codebook, exact zero weights ($w = 0.0$, e.g. from sparsity or padding) are quantized with zero reconstruction error: $\tilde{w} = 0.0$.
